# 06 — AI, Biology, and Quantum Logic for the Prototype

## Biology facts to respect

### Cancer and mutations
Cancer can arise when mutations affect genes that control cell growth, DNA repair, cell death, and signalling pathways. Mutations in oncogenes may increase growth signalling, while mutations in tumour suppressor genes may reduce the cell's ability to control abnormal growth.

### Important gene categories
- **Oncogenes**: Genes that can drive cancer growth when activated, e.g. EGFR, KRAS, ALK.
- **Tumour suppressor genes**: Genes that help control abnormal cell growth, e.g. TP53.
- **DNA repair genes**: Genes involved in genome maintenance, e.g. BRCA1/BRCA2.

### Drug repurposing
Drug repurposing means investigating whether an existing drug or drug class could be useful for a new disease context. In oncology research, this often involves matching altered genes/pathways to known drug targets.

## AI logic

### What AI can do in Phase 1 story
AI can help rank and prioritise:
- Which mutations are likely to be biologically important.
- Which cancer pathways are affected.
- Which drug-target relationships are worth investigating.
- Which hypotheses deserve expert review first.

### What AI cannot claim in Phase 1
AI cannot claim to:
- Diagnose a patient.
- Replace an oncologist.
- Guarantee drug response.
- Prove clinical benefit without validation.

## Quantum logic

### Safe explanation
Quantum machine learning is an emerging field that explores whether quantum or hybrid quantum-classical models can help with complex pattern recognition and optimisation tasks. For this prototype, the quantum module is represented as a future benchmarked compatibility-ranking layer.

### Recommended pitch wording
> We do not assume quantum automatically beats classical AI. Our commercial plan includes benchmarking the hybrid AI-quantum ranking module against classical baselines using retrospective mutation-drug datasets.

### Avoid
- “Quantum supremacy in cancer detection”
- “Guaranteed better than classical AI”
- “Clinically validated quantum diagnosis”

## Prototype ranking explanation

A candidate drug is ranked using:
1. Mutation relevance score
2. Pathway match score
3. Drug-target evidence score
4. Prototype quantum-inspired compatibility score
5. Confidence penalty for uncertain evidence

## Example explanation sentence
> The EGFR inhibitor class is ranked first because the dataset contains a high-confidence EGFR L858R driver mutation, the matched pathway is EGFR/ERBB signalling, and the drug class targets the same pathway. This is a research hypothesis and not a treatment recommendation.
